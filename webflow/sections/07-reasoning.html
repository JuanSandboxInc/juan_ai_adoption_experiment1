Reasoning models and agentic AI

Building on generative AI, reasoning models and agentic AI are the latest developments in the field.

Reasoning models

Reasoning models are LLMs trained to work through a problem in steps before they answer: breaking it down, checking their work and trying another route when something doesn’t add up. This makes them better at maths, coding and planning, but slower and costlier to run. Their “thinking” is still prediction, so it can be wrong.

Examples

  • OpenAI’s o-series models, such as o1 and o3
  • DeepSeek-R1
  • “Thinking” modes in Claude and Gemini

Agentic AI

Agentic AI systems, or AI agents, are given a goal rather than a single question. They plan the steps, use tools such as web search, code or other apps, check the results and keep going until the task is done. Because they act on someone’s behalf, their mistakes can have real consequences. People need to set limits and review what they do.

Examples

  • Coding assistants that write, run and fix software
  • Assistants that browse the web and fill in forms or make bookings
