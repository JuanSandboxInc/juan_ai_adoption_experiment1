What is artificial intelligence?

There are many definitions of artificial intelligence.

In general, AI refers to technologies that let computers do tasks usually associated with human intelligence, such as recognizing patterns, making predictions and generating text, images, audio and video.1,2 It is an umbrella term for many tools with different capabilities.

Today, when people say “AI”, they often mean generative AI. The types of AI are worth telling apart: each works differently and plays a different role in our lives.

Rules written by people

Symbolic AI

Follows rules written by people.3

In practice

IBM Deep Blue

In 1997 Deep Blue beat world chess champion Garry Kasparov. Its programmers told it what each piece is worth, and it scored 200 million possible positions a second using those rules.19

Machine learning

Learns patterns from data.3

In practice

Gmail’s spam filter

Nobody wrote Gmail’s spam rules. It learned what spam looks like from millions of emails people flagged, and Google says it blocks spam with over 99.9% accuracy.20

Generative AI

Creates new content from learned patterns.4

In practice

Image generators such as DALL·E and Stable Diffusion

You describe a picture in words. The tool starts from random noise and shapes it, step by step, into a matching new image. This is called diffusion.

LLMs

Generative AI trained on human language.4

In practice

ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude

They write an answer one word at a time, each time choosing the most likely next word. Because every word is a prediction, an answer can sound confident and still be wrong.
