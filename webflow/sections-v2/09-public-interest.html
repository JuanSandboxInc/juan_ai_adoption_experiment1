Why public-interest AI matters

The choices behind AI technologies, including how models are built, what data they are trained on and where they are deployed, reflect the priorities of the organizations that build them.

These choices impact everyone who uses the technology or is affected by it, yet most of those people had no say in them.

Public-interest AI offers an alternative path for developing and deploying AI, one that centres the needs of communities. It envisions AI that is designed, built and used to serve the public’s long-term wellbeing and safeguard people’s democratic rights.

Public AI, a concept supported by a wide community of experts and practitioners, calls for as much public control as possible over as many parts of the AI stack as possible: compute power, data, the models themselves and the apps that make AI accessible to the public. Public AI systems should be accessible, interoperable and transparent, accountable to the public and governed by democratic principles.

Advancing public-interest AI

Sandbox Labs is part of a global movement supporting public-interest AI and Public AI. With the UPF-Barcelona School of Management, and hosted at Norrsken House Barcelona, we combine our expertise in digital technology, education and policy in the Public-Interest AI Accelerator: a new initiative that builds the skills of today’s and tomorrow’s leaders so the future of AI benefits people and societies at large.

The pilot edition of the Accelerator ran in 2026. Over nine weeks, participants worked with experts in law and digital rights, health, policy, computer science and neuroscience. They explored AI’s role in geopolitics and supply chains, the legal and human-rights questions it raises, what ethical governance looks like and how public-interest AI is built and implemented in the real world.

The Public-Interest AI Accelerator is designed to help participants engage critically with AI and answer key questions: Who benefits? Who carries the risks? What trade-offs must be transparent? How can public policy leaders make sound decisions in an AI world?
